If you are an Iraqi business owner looking at accounting and ERP software in 2026, two options will end up on your shortlist: **Zoho Books** (and the broader Zoho One bundle), and the **Kurdish ERP**. This article is a side-by-side comparison written by the Kurdish ERP team — we are biased, but we will try to be honest about where Zoho is the stronger choice. Read this alongside a Zoho-team perspective and your own trial of both.

## Where Zoho Books wins

**Maturity and breadth.** Zoho Books has been in market since 2011. The product is polished, the integration catalogue is vast (over 50 Zoho apps and hundreds of third-party connectors), and the support is professional. If you need an integration with a global payment processor, an e-commerce platform, or a CRM that is *not* Iraqi, Zoho has the connector.

**Multi-country compliance.** Zoho Books supports tax regimes in 17+ countries out of the box. If your business operates across Iraq, the GCC, and Turkey, the same Zoho Books tenant handles all of them with regional editions.

**Bank feeds in supported regions.** In countries where Zoho has direct bank-feed integrations, daily reconciliation is automatic. (Iraq is **not** currently in this list — see below.)

**Professional accountants know it.** Many Iraqi accountants and audit firms have Zoho certifications. If you outsource your books to a Sulaymaniyah or Baghdad accounting firm, they may already know Zoho.

## Where Kurdish ERP wins

**Kurdish (Sorani) UI throughout, not a Google-translated layer.** Every screen, every report, every error message is native Kurdish. Arabic and English are equal first-class citizens. Zoho Books is available in Arabic with RTL but Kurdish support is partial at best.

**Iraqi tax codes as first-class concepts.** Withholding tax at 3% / 5% / 2% is built into every supplier record. VAT registration vs not-registered drives invoice behaviour automatically. The chart of accounts ships pre-loaded with the Iraqi 5-digit structure. e-Fakhata XML generation, signing, and MoF submission are end-to-end automatic on the Pro plan. None of this is in Zoho Books out of the box; you would build or buy a plugin.

**Iraqi power and internet reality.** The Kurdish ERP POS runs offline on IndexedDB. Zoho's POS is online-first. In an Iraqi shop where power blinks ten times a day, this is the difference between a working till and a paper-receipt fallback.

**IQD-native.** Iraqi Dinar is the primary currency, not a multi-currency add-on. Denominations are first-class (50K, 25K, 10K, 5K, 1K, 500, 250). The CBI daily rate is auto-fetched for IQD↔USD conversion. Zoho supports IQD as one of many currencies but does not have IQD-specific affordances like the denomination breakdown for cash-drawer reconciliation.

**Bank-feed alternative for Iraqi banks.** Since Iraqi bank-feed APIs are limited, we ship a CSV import + reconciliation tool that handles the common Iraqi bank statement formats. Not magic, but better than not having it.

**Hardware compatibility matrix for Iraqi-market printers.** Epson TM-T20III, Xprinter XP-T80A, Bixolon SRP-330II are all tested and dialect-mapped. Zoho's POS relies on browser-driver paths that may or may not match what your supplier sells you.

**Pricing in IQD.** Annual plans billed in IQD, with USD as an option for cross-border buyers. Zoho is billed in USD globally.

**Support in Kurdish, Arabic, English.** WhatsApp, email, and phone support from a team that speaks the language your shopkeeper does.

## When to pick Zoho Books

Pick Zoho Books if:

- You operate in multiple countries and Iraq is one of several.
- You need a specific third-party integration that Zoho has and the Kurdish ERP does not (verify with both teams).
- Your accountant is Zoho-certified and you want to standardize.
- You can live without offline POS, WHT automation, e-Fakhata automation, and the Iraq-specific affordances.

## When to pick Kurdish ERP

Pick Kurdish ERP if:

- You operate primarily in Iraq.
- You want Kurdish-language UI for your staff (this is non-negotiable for many operators).
- You need offline POS that works during power outages.
- You will be required to comply with e-Fakhata in 2026-2028.
- You want WHT, VAT, IQD, and CBI rates handled natively.
- You want pricing in IQD with support that speaks your language.
